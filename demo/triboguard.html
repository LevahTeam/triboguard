A1  /  the trap

Two drugs.
One number.

Drug A kills cells. Drug B only stops them dividing. Measure either with a standard viability assay and you get the same answer — not roughly the same, exactly the same. The difference is hiding somewhere nobody looks.

Both populations grow at birth minus death — 0.030 per hour — so their averages trace the same curve to twelve decimal places. What differs is how much replicate wells disagree: that depends on birth plus death, and Drug A’s is exactly twice Drug B’s. An assay that averages its wells throws the answer away.

The two wells above are a live simulation, one well of 24 in each arm; press “Run again” and the counts change but the pattern does not. The chart is the exact math.

A3  /  the 2022 study

Seven conclusions. Two hold.

A student study reported that a Tribonema algae extract cut the MTS signal of RAW264.7 cells by 40%. Each conclusion drawn from it was checked against what the experiment could actually measure. Nothing here says the extract does not work — the reported effect stands.

    A4  /  selectivity

    “Spares normal cells” — or just doesn’t divide?

    “% cytotoxicity” is measured against an untreated well read the same day. A dividing control gains cells all day; a resting one doesn’t. So a drug that only slows division scores in the cancer arm and nowhere else.

    A6  /  what a count can say

    A count is a range, not a number.

    A cell the software can’t see counts exactly like a cell that’s gone. So a drop in the count only pins down how many cells are really gone to a range — and when that range covers both “nothing” and “a real effect”, the honest answer is to abstain.

    What this is, and isn’t

    Every number on this page is produced by the project’s own scripts and read from its saved results. The damage in A5 is synthetic, each cell line’s images come from one well, and no Tribonema images have been collected — nothing here is evidence about the extract itself.
